1
Lift at Belle Mont Resort
BASIC
Problem: A lift and its passengers at Belle Mont Sanctuary Resort have a total mass of 400 kg. The lift is supported by a single cable. Find the tension in the cable when:
  • (a) the lift is at rest
  • (b) the lift is moving upward at constant speed of 2 m/s
  • (c) the lift is accelerating upwards at 0.8 m/s²
  • (d) the lift is accelerating downwards at 0.6 m/s²

📐 Solution Strategy

Given:

• Mass m = 400 kg

• g = 10 m/s²

• Weight W = mg = 400 × 10 = 4000 N

Choose: Upward as positive direction ↑

Free Body Diagram

Forces on Lift:

↑ Tension T (upward - positive)

↓ Weight 4000 N (downward - negative)

a
At Rest (a = 0)

When at rest, the lift is in equilibrium. Forces must balance.

ΣF = 0
T - W = 0
T - 4000 = 0
T = 4000 N
b
Moving at Constant Speed (a = 0)

Constant velocity means zero acceleration! Still in equilibrium.

T - W = ma
T - 4000 = 400(0)
T - 4000 = 0
T = 4000 N

Note: Same as at rest! Speed doesn't matter, only acceleration.

c
Accelerating Upward (a = +0.8 m/s²)

Net upward force needed to accelerate upward.

F = ma
T - W = ma
T - 4000 = 400(0.8)
T - 4000 = 320
T = 4320 N
T = 4320 N

Tension INCREASES when accelerating upward - you feel heavier!

d
Accelerating Downward (a = -0.6 m/s²)

Acceleration is downward (negative in our convention).

T - W = ma
T - 4000 = 400(-0.6)
T - 4000 = -240
T = 3760 N
T = 3760 N

Tension DECREASES when accelerating downward - you feel lighter!

✓ Final Answers

(a) T = 4000 N
(b) T = 4000 N
(c) T = 4320 N
(d) T = 3760 N

🔑 Key Learning Points

  • At rest or constant velocity → a = 0 → Tension equals weight
  • Accelerating up → Tension > Weight (you feel heavier)
  • Accelerating down → Tension < Weight (you feel lighter)
  • Always choose a positive direction and stick to it!
2
Helicopter Rescue at Nevis Peak
INTERMEDIATE
Problem: A stationary helicopter hovers above Nevis Peak, raising two hikers connected by a light inextensible rope. Hiker A (top) has mass 90 kg and hiker B (bottom) has mass 70 kg. The helicopter applies an upward force of 1800 N to hiker A.
  • (a) Calculate the acceleration of the two hikers
  • (b) Find the tension in the rope connecting the hikers

📐 Solution Strategy

Given:

• Mass of A: mA = 90 kg

• Mass of B: mB = 70 kg

• Applied force on A: F = 1800 N

• g = 10 m/s²

Choose: Upward as positive direction ↑

Key insight: Both hikers have SAME acceleration (connected by inextensible rope)

Free Body Diagrams

Hiker A (90 kg):

↑ Applied force: 1800 N

↓ Weight: 900 N

↓ Tension: T

Hiker B (70 kg):

↑ Tension: T

↓ Weight: 700 N

1
Write Equations for Each Hiker

For Hiker A:

ΣF = mAa
1800 - 900 - T = 90a
900 - T = 90a ... ①

For Hiker B:

ΣF = mBa
T - 700 = 70a ... ②
a
Find Acceleration - Add Equations

Add equation ① and equation ②:

(900 - T) + (T - 700) = 90a + 70a
900 - 700 = 160a
200 = 160a
a = 200/160 = 1.25 m/s²
a = 1.25 m/s²

Notice how tension T cancelled out! This is the benefit of adding equations.

b
Find Tension - Substitute into Equation ②

Use equation ② with a = 1.25 m/s²:

T - 700 = 70(1.25)
T - 700 = 87.5
T = 787.5 N
T = 787.5 N

Verification: Check with equation ①:

900 - 787.5 = 90(1.25)
112.5 = 112.5 ✓

✓ Final Answers

(a) Acceleration: a = 1.25 m/s²
(b) Rope tension: T = 787.5 N

🔑 Key Learning Points

  • Connected objects have SAME acceleration if rope is inextensible
  • Draw separate free body diagram for each object
  • Add equations to eliminate unknown (tension) when finding acceleration
  • Substitute back to find the eliminated variable
  • Always verify your answer by checking both equations!
3
Water Taxi at Christophe Harbour
BASIC
Problem: A water taxi at Christophe Harbour has mass 500 kg. Its engine provides a driving force of 2500 N while it experiences water resistance of 500 N. Starting from rest:
  • (a) Calculate the acceleration
  • (b) Find its velocity after 8 seconds
  • (c) Calculate the distance traveled in 8 seconds

📐 Solution Strategy

Given:

• Mass m = 500 kg

• Driving force Fdrive = 2500 N

• Resistance Fresist = 500 N

• Initial velocity u = 0 (starts from rest)

• Time t = 8 s

Choose: Forward (direction of motion) as positive →

a
Find Acceleration

First, find the net (resultant) force:

Fnet = Fdrive - Fresist
Fnet = 2500 - 500 = 2000 N

Apply Newton's Second Law:

F = ma
2000 = 500a
a = 2000/500 = 4 m/s²
a = 4 m/s²
b
Find Velocity After 8 Seconds

Use kinematic equation: v = u + at

v = u + at
v = 0 + 4(8)
v = 32 m/s
v = 32 m/s
c
Find Distance Traveled

Use kinematic equation: s = ut + ½at²

s = ut + ½at²
s = 0(8) + ½(4)(8²)
s = 0 + ½(4)(64)
s = 2(64) = 128 m
s = 128 m

Alternative method: Use v² = u² + 2as to verify

32² = 0² + 2(4)s
1024 = 8s
s = 128 m ✓

✓ Final Answers

(a) a = 4 m/s²
(b) v = 32 m/s
(c) s = 128 m

🔑 Key Learning Points

  • Net force = Driving force - Resistance
  • Use F = ma to find acceleration from forces
  • Then use kinematic equations for velocity and displacement
  • Always verify using alternative equation when possible

Crate with Friction at Port Zante
BASIC
Problem: A shipping crate of mass 80 kg sits on the dock at Port Zante. A dock worker pulls it horizontally with a rope at tension 240 N. The crate experiences friction of 80 N opposing motion.
  • (a) Calculate the acceleration
  • (b) Starting from rest, find its velocity after 5 seconds
  • (c) How far has it moved in 5 seconds?

📐 Solution Strategy

Given:

• Mass m = 80 kg

• Tension T = 240 N (pulling force)

• Friction f = 80 N (opposes motion)

• Initial velocity u = 0

• Time t = 5 s

a
Find Acceleration

Horizontal forces (choosing forward as positive):

Fnet = T - f = 240 - 80 = 160 N
F = ma
160 = 80a
a = 2 m/s²
a = 2 m/s²
b
Find Velocity After 5 Seconds
v = u + at
v = 0 + 2(5)
v = 10 m/s
v = 10 m/s
c
Find Distance
s = ut + ½at²
s = 0 + ½(2)(25)
s = 25 m
s = 25 m

✓ Final Answers

(a) a = 2 m/s²
(b) v = 10 m/s
(c) s = 25 m

🔑 Key Learning Points

  • Friction always opposes motion (negative if forward is positive)
  • Net force = Applied force - Friction
  • Then apply standard F = ma and kinematics

Connected Boats at Frigate Bay
INTERMEDIATE
Problem: Two boats are connected by a light rope. Boat A (front, 250 kg) has a motor providing 1800 N thrust. Boat B (rear, 150 kg) is being towed. The system experiences 600 N total water resistance.
  • (a) Find the acceleration of the boats
  • (b) Calculate the tension in the connecting rope

📐 Solution Strategy

Given:

• Mass of A: mA = 250 kg

• Mass of B: mB = 150 kg

• Thrust on A: F = 1800 N

• Total resistance: 600 N

Key insight: Connected boats have same acceleration

a
Find Acceleration - Consider Whole System

For the system as a whole (tension is internal, cancels out):

Total mass = 250 + 150 = 400 kg
Net force = 1800 - 600 = 1200 N
F = ma
1200 = 400a
a = 3 m/s²
a = 3 m/s²
b
Find Tension - Consider Boat B Alone

Assume resistance distributed proportionally by mass:

Resistance on B = 600 × (150/400) = 225 N

For boat B (only tension pulls it forward):

T - 225 = 150 × 3
T - 225 = 450
T = 675 N
T = 675 N

✓ Final Answers

(a) a = 3 m/s²
(b) T = 675 N

🔑 Key Learning Points

  • For connected system: consider as whole to find acceleration
  • Tension is INTERNAL force - cancels when considering system
  • To find tension: isolate one object and apply F = ma
  • Check answer: tension pulls B forward, pulls A backward

Package on Smooth Incline at Sandy Point
INTERMEDIATE
Problem: A 10 kg package slides down a smooth incline at Sandy Point. The incline makes an angle of 30° with the horizontal. Taking g = 10 m/s²:
  • (a) Find the component of weight down the slope
  • (b) Calculate the acceleration down the slope
  • (c) If released from rest, what is its speed after sliding 5 m?

📐 Solution Strategy

Given:

• Mass m = 10 kg

• Angle θ = 30°

• g = 10 m/s²

• Smooth surface (no friction)

• Distance s = 5 m

Choose: Down the slope as positive

Force Components

Weight W = mg = 100 N acts vertically downward

Component down slope: W sin θ = mg sin 30°

Component perpendicular: W cos θ = mg cos 30°

a
Component Down Slope
Fdown = mg sin θ
Fdown = 10 × 10 × sin 30°
Fdown = 100 × 0.5
Fdown = 50 N
Component down slope = 50 N
b
Acceleration Down Slope

Since smooth (no friction), only component down slope causes acceleration:

F = ma
50 = 10a
a = 5 m/s²
a = 5 m/s²

Alternative: a = g sin θ = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m/s²

c
Speed After 5 m

Use v² = u² + 2as with u = 0:

v² = 0² + 2(5)(5)
v² = 50
v = √50 = 7.07 m/s
v ≈ 7.1 m/s

✓ Final Answers

(a) Component down slope = 50 N
(b) a = 5 m/s²
(c) v = 7.1 m/s

🔑 Key Learning Points

  • On incline: resolve weight into components parallel and perpendicular to slope
  • Component down slope = mg sin θ
  • For smooth surface: acceleration = g sin θ (independent of mass!)
  • Normal reaction = mg cos θ

Three Hanging Masses
ADVANCED
Problem: Three masses hang vertically connected by light strings: A (5 kg) at top, B (3 kg) in middle, C (2 kg) at bottom. A string above A pulls the system upward with force 120 N. Find: (a) acceleration, (b) tension between A-B, (c) tension between B-C.
a
System Acceleration
Total mass = 10 kg, Total weight = 100 N
120 - 100 = 10a
a = 2 m/s²
b
Tension T₁ (between A and B)

Consider B+C together (mass = 5 kg, weight = 50 N):

T₁ - 50 = 5(2)
T₁ = 60 N
c
Tension T₂ (between B and C)

Consider C alone (mass = 2 kg, weight = 20 N):

T₂ - 20 = 2(2)
T₂ = 24 N

✓ Final Answers

(a) a = 2 m/s²
(b) T₁ = 60 N
(c) T₂ = 24 N

CFBC Shuttle Bus
INTERMEDIATE
Problem: A 1500 kg CFBC shuttle bus brakes from 25 m/s with braking force 4500 N and rolling resistance 1500 N. Find: (a) deceleration, (b) stopping time, (c) stopping distance.

Total retarding force = 4500 + 1500 = 6000 N

(a) a = -6000/1500 = -4 m/s²
(b) 0 = 25 + (-4)t → t = 6.25 s
(c) v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 625 - 8s → s = 78.125 m

✓ Final Answers

(a) Deceleration = 4 m/s²
(b) Stopping time = 6.25 s
(c) Stopping distance = 78.1 m
